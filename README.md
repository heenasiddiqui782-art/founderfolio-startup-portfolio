## Project overview

This project turns one startup concept into a complete entrepreneurial portfolio and a 12-slide investor-style pitch deck. It demonstrates the ability to:

- identify a real customer problem and define the target customer
- plan customer and market research
- size the market with top-down and bottom-up methods
- analyse competitors honestly
- design a value proposition, business model, MVP and pricing
- build a go-to-market plan and a 12-month financial model with three scenarios
- identify risks and set milestones
- present the work as a professional pitch deck and pitch scripts

The main deliverables are the **project report** (``) and the **pitch deck** (`/pitch-deck`).

## Startup concept

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | FounderFolio (working name; trademark and domain not yet checked) |
| Tagline | Your story, investor-ready, and honest about the evidence. |
| Industry | Startup enablement / SaaS |
| Product | Web tool that guides founders through structured inputs, drafts wording with AI, tags every claim as Verified, Assumption or Projection, and exports a 12-slide deck and a shareable portfolio page |
| Geography | India (English first; Tier 1 and Tier 2 cities) |
| Business model | Freemium subscription plus institutional licences |

**One-line pitch:** We help student and first-time founders in India turn an idea into an investor-ready portfolio and pitch deck by providing a guided, AI-assisted builder that labels every claim as evidence or assumption, resulting in faster, more credible pitches without agency costs.

## Business problem

Student and first-time founders struggle to present their ventures credibly because they lack structure, design skill and evidence discipline. The result is weak pitches, missed opportunities and scattered portfolios. Today they use general design templates, copied decks, AI drafts or costly freelancers.

> The severity and frequency of this problem are **assumptions** that must be validated through customer interviews.

## Target customer

- **Primary:** student and first-time founders in India (E-Cell members, competition participants, incubator applicants)
- **Secondary:** freelancers, consultants and solo founders who need a credible portfolio
- **Early adopters:** active E-Cell members who pitch often
- **Buyer vs user:** individuals buy and use. For institutional plans, the college, E-Cell or incubator buys and students use.

Three synthetic personas (Aarav, Meera, Rohan) are in the report. They are illustrative and will be replaced by real interview insights.

## Competitor analysis

Direct: Slidebean, Canva, Pitch, Gamma, Beautiful.ai. Indirect: Notion, PowerPoint and Google Slides, DIY portfolio sites, design agencies.

Competitors are stronger than FounderFolio today in design, templates, brand and resources. The proposed whitespace is an affordable, founder-specialised, India-aware tool with built-in evidence labelling. Competitor prices must be checked on live vendor pages before use; unverified prices are marked "verify" in the report.

## Value proposition

Provisional choice: *the builder that shows investors which of your claims are verified and which are assumptions*, with a free-to-start, India-focused entry. This needs validation; if founders do not value evidence labels, the angle should change.

## MVP

- **Core promise:** a structured, evidence-labelled deck draft in under 30 minutes
- **Included:** guided input wizard, AI-assisted wording, evidence tags, 2 templates, PPTX/PDF export
- **Excluded for now:** payments, team collaboration, analytics, regional languages
- **Test:** concierge or no-code MVP with 20 to 30 student founders over 6 to 8 weeks
- **Target success metrics (not results):** 60% complete a deck, 40% would reuse it, 10% willing to pay

## Go-to-market strategy

`PROPOSED STRATEGY`

1. **Validate** (months 0-2): interviews and survey
2. **MVP** (months 2-4): concierge test with 20 to 30 founders
3. **Early adopters** (months 4-6): first paying users via campus communities
4. **Launch** (months 6-9): SEO, content, referrals
5. **Growth** (months 9-24): institutional pilots

## Financial model

`PROJECTION` built on `ASSUMPTION` inputs. Not actual results.

| Item | Base case |
|---|---|
| Paying customers at month 12 | 540 |
| Revenue at month 12 | Rs 2,26,800 a month |
| Contribution margin | Rs 330 per customer a month (about 79%) |
| CAC (modelled) | about Rs 830 |
| LTV (6% monthly churn assumed) | about Rs 5,500 |
| Break-even (fixed costs only) | about 155 customers |
| Monthly profit reached | month 9 (about 270 customers) |
| Peak cash need | about Rs 2.8 lakh (base); about Rs 3.5 lakh (conservative) |

Three scenarios (conservative, base, optimistic) are in the report. The proposed funding need is about **Rs 4.0 lakh** including contingency, which is bootstrap or grant scale rather than a venture round. The LTV:CAC ratio looks high because early assumptions are generous and must be replaced with measured data.

## Key learnings

- A good deck cannot fix a weak idea.
- Evidence matters more than presentation.
- Assumptions, projections, validation and actual traction must be kept separate.
- AI is an analytical assistant, not a source of facts. Statistics, prices and competitor details must be verified.

## Tools used

AI assistants (drafting and structuring, with all facts checked by the author), spreadsheets for financial modelling, presentation software, Word, and GitHub for documentation.

## Limitations

- No customer evidence yet; no interviews or survey completed
- Market percentages, prices, CAC, churn and customer numbers are assumptions
- Some competitor pricing and feedback are unverified
- Solo-founder concept; no working product built

## Future scope

**Real business validation:** complete interviews and survey, launch a landing page and waitlist, build a live MVP, acquire first test users, validate pricing, generate first revenue, measure CAC and retention, improve unit economics, build an investor data room, and explore funding only when evidence supports it.

**Presentation improvements:** product mockups, a pitch video, a polished portfolio website, and startup competitions.
